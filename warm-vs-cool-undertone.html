Warm vs cool undertone: 4 easy ways to tell

Short answer: if the veins on your wrist look green, gold jewellery suits you and you tan easily, you're probably warm. If your veins look blue or purple, silver suits you and you burn easily, you're probably cool. A mix of both means neutral.

Your skin tone is how light or dark your skin is, and it changes with a tan. Your undertone is the colour underneath, and it stays the same. Undertone is what decides whether golden or blue-based colours flatter you.

1. The vein test

In daylight, look at the veins on your inner wrist. Green or olive suggests warm. Blue or purple suggests cool. If you honestly can't decide, you're likely neutral. This is harder on deep skin, so lean on the other tests too.

2. The jewellery test

Hold gold and then silver against your skin, or think about which one people compliment. Gold looking better suggests warm, silver suggests cool, and both suggests neutral.

3. The sun test

Tan easily, rarely burn: often warm. Burn or go pink first: often cool. This works best on lighter skin; deeper skin rarely burns, whatever the undertone.

4. The white paper test

Hold pure white paper next to your bare face in daylight. If your skin looks yellow, golden or peachy by comparison you're warm, pink or rosy means cool, and neither means neutral.

What to wear for your undertone

Warm

Earthy and golden colours: terracotta, mustard, olive, rust, coral, teal, cream and camel. Gold jewellery.

Cool

Blue-based and jewel colours: sapphire, emerald, berry, lavender, crisp white, grey and navy. Silver jewellery.

Neutral

Most colours, especially mid-tones: dusty rose, jade, denim, plum and soft white. Gold or silver.

Quick answers

Am I warm or cool toned?

Check your wrist veins in daylight (green = warm, blue or purple = cool), see whether gold or silver suits you, and notice if you tan or burn. Mixed results usually mean neutral.

Can you have a neutral undertone?

Yes. Neutral undertones show signs of both warm and cool and can wear most colours, especially muted and mid-tone shades.

Does your undertone change with a tan?

No. A tan changes your skin tone (how dark you are) but not your undertone.
